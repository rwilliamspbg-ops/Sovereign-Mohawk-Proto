The real mathematical content is `RenyiDivergence_product_add`: Rényi divergence tensorizes exactly over independent products (`D_α(p1×p2‖q1×q2) = D_α(p1‖q1) + D_α(p2‖q2)`), proved directly via `Real.mul_rpow`/`Fintype.sum_prod_type`/`Finset.sum_mul_sum`/`Real.log_mul`, not asserted; `RDP_sequential_composition` is that equality combined with the two individual bounds via `linarith`. A feasibility scout (grepping the pinned Mathlib source for conditional-probability/kernel machinery — `Mathlib.Probability.Kernel`, `condexp`, etc. — all real and non-stub, but with zero Rényi/KL/f-divergence content anywhere in Mathlib to build on) found that the *expectation-based* chain rule previously cited here, `D_α(p(x,y)‖q(x,y)) = D_α(p(x)‖q(x)) + 𝔼_x[D_α(p(y|x)‖q(y|x))]`, is **not actually a valid identity for Rényi divergence at general order** — it's a KL-divergence (α→1) fact; working the same factoring technique `RenyiDivergence_product_add` uses out for genuinely `x`-dependent conditional distributions does not collapse to an expectation over `x`. The statement that **is** provable, and matches the standard literature result (Mironov, "Rényi Differential Privacy," 2017, Prop. 1), replaces the expectation with a uniform/worst-case bound: given `p2, q2 : α → β → ℝ` (M2's conditional output distributions, genuinely indexed by M1's realized output `x` — not a relabeled independent case) and `∀ x, D_α(p2 x‖q2 x) ≤ eps2` (the bound holds *for every possible `x`*, not just the one realized — necessary since the joint bound must hold regardless of which `x` occurs), the joint output is `(α, eps1+eps2)`-RDP. Proof: factor each joint term as `RenyiDivergence_product_add` does, split the double sum into an outer sum over `x` of inner sums over `y`, bound each inner sum pointwise via the new `RenyiDivergence_sum_le_of_bound` (inverts the `log`/coefficient step to turn a log-space RDP bound into a linear-space sum bound), sum the pointwise bound, then invert the log/coefficient step once more. Setting `p2 x`/`q2 x` constant across `x` recovers `RDP_sequential_composition`'s exact statement, confirming this is a strict generalization rather than a relabeling. (The previously-removed `Theorem2RDP_ChainRule.lean`, which attempted the — as it turns out, incorrect — chain-rule route, did not compile and had nothing usable to build on; this proof does not reuse or resemble it.)
